# Passive Blocks Weekly — Passive Income × Growth

**Week of Jul 10 – Jul 16, 2026** · *Building durable income and long-term wealth across every rail.*

---

One cool inflation print did a lot of work this week. June CPI landed soft, a July rate hike quietly fell off the table, and risk assets exhaled — ether jumped nearly 7% in a session, bitcoin firmed, and the growth complex ran. It felt like the all-clear. It wasn't. Relief is not a regime change, and a single data point that reprices *hike* odds from 42% to 17% is not the same as the Fed handing out cheap money again. The more durable story this week wasn't the tape at all — it was the widening gap between businesses printing real cash (TSMC's record margins, memory sold out through next year, nuclear utilities with signed offtake) and narratives printing *activity* (an "agentic economy" moving $24 million on 75 million transactions, neoclouds financed by their own chip supplier). Our through-line for the week: underwrite the *source* of every yield and every growth dollar. Real cash flows compound. Narrative volume unwinds.

## The Regime: relief, not a pivot

The number that mattered: June headline CPI cooled to [3.5% year-over-year from 4.2%](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/stock-market-news-july-15-111400318.html), with the monthly figure actually *negative* and core easing to 2.6%. That was enough to quiet the part of the market that had spent June bracing for another Fed *hike* — odds of a July increase collapsed, and traders now put ~85% on the Fed simply holding at 3.50–3.75%. Treasury yields slipped, the two-year leading the move lower, and the dollar softened at the margin.

Here's the part worth sitting with. For weeks the working assumption in the research we track has been a tightening, higher-for-longer regime with a firm dollar — the kind of backdrop that flushes the most speculative assets first (bitcoin and gold both took double-digit hits earlier in the quarter) while credit spreads sit historically tight with little cushion. This week's print doesn't overturn that. It relieves the *tail* — the hike scare — without delivering the easing that a genuine risk-on regime needs. The dollar is still firm. QT has run its course but no fresh liquidity is being injected. Credit is still the last domino standing. What changed is that the market stopped pricing the worst case, and in a jumpy tape that's enough for a bounce.

For a portfolio that lives on income *and* growth, the read is straightforward: this is an environment to keep getting paid while you wait, and to be picky about which growth you underwrite. Cash and short bills still yield north of 4%, which sets a real hurdle every income position has to clear. And when the speculative end rallies on relief, it's a chance to check whether the thing you own throws off cash — or just narrative.

## Crypto rails × AI: the plumbing is real, most of the "volume" isn't

The agentic-payments standard had its biggest validation week yet. Ripple joined Visa and Mastercard in [backing x402](https://www.coindesk.com/tech/2026/07/15/visa-mastercard-and-ripple-join-the-standard-letting-ai-agents-pay-in-stablecoins), the open standard that lets software agents settle in stablecoins over plain HTTP, and the Linux Foundation's x402 body now counts 40-odd members. On Base, agentic transfers [crossed 20 million in a single 90-day window](https://cryptobriefing.com/base-agentic-payments-20-million-transfers/). This is the part of crypto we're most constructive on structurally: a settlement layer that nobody owns, that any agent can plug into, becoming the default way machines pay machines. That's infrastructure, not a coin.

But lead with the data, because the data is sobering. Those same x402 rails moved roughly **$24 million across ~75 million transactions** over the past month — sub-dollar payments, overwhelmingly. And when Artemis re-ran its methodology for flagging synthetic activity, it found the ratio of real to "gamed" x402 transactions running close to [1:1](https://cryptoslate.com/tiny-x402-payments-expose-gap-holding-ai-agents-back/), with the vast majority of some chains' all-time agentic volume classified as gamed. The protocol's real dollar volume actually *collapsed* around 77% from its late-2025 peak. There's also a design problem underneath the hype: a human approving each sub-cent call at a 5-to-15-second cadence can burn more in time-value than the payment is worth — the [approval gap](https://letsdatascience.com/news/x402-reveals-approval-gap-in-agentic-micropayments-761bf8e7) that frameworks like Google's AP2 mandates are racing to close. Skepticism from a16z and Artemis on the headline transaction counts is the correct posture here.

The quieter, higher-signal tell sits in the stablecoins themselves. Total stablecoin supply is holding around [$314 billion](https://about.artemis.ai/solutions/stablecoin) even as more than $10 billion drained out since May into memecoin churn — dry powder parked on-chain rather than fleeing. Washington is leaning in: five US regulators proposed [bank-grade KYC rules](https://cryptonews.com/news/crypto-news-july-13-stablecoin-market-cap-memecoin-clarity-act-bitcoin-ethereum-price/) for issuers under the GENIUS Act, and the CLARITY market-structure bill keeps advancing. Regulated dollar rails are the real prize. The agent-payment fireworks are a preview of demand that hasn't arrived yet — own the settlement layer's growth, discount the transaction-count theater.

## DeFi & staking yield: underwrite the source, not the APY

The most useful launch of the week came from Aave, which rolled out [Stable Vaults](https://cryptobriefing.com/aave-stable-vaults-stablecoin-yield/) — fixed-rate stablecoin yield that fintechs, wallets and exchanges can wire in through a single integration rather than building DeFi plumbing themselves. It's a direct shot at Morpho, whose curated vaults already sit on Coinbase and Robinhood front-ends holding hundreds of millions. The competitive heat is good for depositors and telling about where this is going: on-chain yield is quietly becoming a back-end utility that consumer apps resell.

The numbers, and where the yield comes from, matter more than the marketing. Blue-chip on-chain lending — Aave, Morpho, Spark, Sky — [pays roughly 3.5% to 9%](https://eco.com/support/en/articles/14800882-best-defi-lending-protocols-2026-tvl-rates-risk) on stablecoins right now, with USDC supply on Aave in the 3–6% band and Morpho's isolated markets reaching higher because they concentrate borrow demand instead of diluting it. Crucially, that yield is *real* — it's paid by leveraged borrowers who want dollars, not conjured from a token-emissions faucet. Contrast that with ETH staking, where native rewards have [compressed to about 2.6–2.8%](https://www.kucoin.com/blog/ethereum-staking-in-2026-yield-trends-validator-queue-dynamics-and-mev-impact-exlained) as roughly a third of supply now stakes, and where BlackRock's staking ETF passes through closer to [2.0–2.6% net](https://www.altrady.com/blog/cryptocurrency/eth-staking-etf-blackrock-ethb-2026) after an 18% fee skim. Staking yield is really a claim on network security plus ETH's own price path; stablecoin lending is a claim on borrower demand. Neither is free money, and the honest comparison is a lending rate a bit above cash for smart-contract and depeg risk. Get paid for the risk you're actually taking, and know which one you're taking.

## Dividends & income equities: when cash pays 4%, your income has to earn it

With T-bills still yielding north of 4%, every dividend position has to justify itself against a risk-free alternative — and that's exactly the frame we like for this pillar. The clean example is the SCHD-versus-JEPQ debate that resurfaced this week. Schwab's dividend grower [yields around 3.3%](https://www.infnits.com/blog/schd-vs-jepq-dividend-etf-comparison) but has compounded its payout at roughly 10–12% a year, in tax-friendly qualified dividends. JPMorgan's Nasdaq covered-call fund throws off a headline 9–11%, paid monthly — but it's largely options premium taxed as ordinary income, and the strategy caps your upside in exactly the growth tape that just reignited.

The contrarian read: in a higher-for-longer world, the reflex is to grab the fattest current yield, and that reflex is usually wrong for anyone who doesn't need the cash today. A 3.3% yield growing double-digits overtakes a static 11% payout on total return over a surprisingly short horizon, and it does it without surrendering the equity upside a covered-call sleeve sells away. High current income has a place — for spenders, not compounders. Rate-sensitive income names like the monthly-paying REITs also deserve a fresh look now that hike risk has faded, though a firm dollar and tight credit argue for quality over reach. The names we follow here skew to durable dividend growers and cash-rich value; the yield-trap end of the market is where a higher-for-longer regime does its damage.

## Growth: TSMC's margins are the signal; the neoclouds' financing is the warning

If you wanted one number to cut through the AI-bubble noise, TSMC handed it over on Wednesday: a [record quarter](https://www.tradingkey.com/analysis/stocks/us-stocks/262033904-tsmc-q2-profit-77-percent-record-hightradingkey) with revenue up ~34% to $40.2 billion, net profit up 77%, and gross margin at a fat 67.7% — with high-performance computing now two-thirds of the business. Management called AI demand "extremely robust" and guided full-year growth above 30%. This is the picks-and-shovels thesis working: the foundry that everyone's silicon runs through is printing cash at rising margins, not chasing volume at any cost. Memory tells the same story from a different seat — Micron has [sold out its entire 2026 HBM output](https://www.thestreet.com/investing/stocks/micron-stock-jumps-as-investors-look-beyond-gpus-in-ai-chip-trade), HBM4 is ramping for Nvidia's next platform, and analysts keep raising targets on tightening supply. The capital-light designers and the genuinely supply-constrained suppliers are where the compute super-cycle pays.

The other side of the buildout is where we'd keep the skeptic's hat on. Power is the real bottleneck, and the way to play it is signed offtake, not story stocks: [Constellation's 55-gigawatt fleet](https://www.sahmcapital.com/news/content/3-nuclear-energy-stocks-powering-ai-data-center-demand-2026-07-09) with long-term PPAs to Microsoft and Meta, Vistra's contracts with Amazon and Meta, GE Vernova's grid-and-turbine backlog. Those are cash-flow contracts, not hope. The neoclouds are the warning label. After Meta signaled it would build its own AI cloud, CoreWeave and Nebius [cratered](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/nebius-coreweave-iren-tumble-meta-162444225.html), and the deeper unease is structural: the sector is increasingly [vendor-financed](https://www.forbes.com/sites/janakirammsv/2026/07/03/why-the-neocloud-gold-rush-is-now-vendor-financed/), with Nvidia itself rolling out revenue-sharing and credit support so providers can deploy GPUs without carrying the full capex. When the chip supplier is also underwriting its customers' balance sheets to buy the chips, the demand looks a lot more circular than the backlog headlines suggest. We'll take the toll-taker's margin over the levered buildout's IOU.

## How we're playing it

The posture is a barbell, and this week sharpened both ends. On the income end: real yield we can underwrite — on-chain stablecoin lending paid by actual borrowers, dividend *growers* that compound faster than a static high payout, cash and short bills still doing real work at 4%-plus while the regime stays firm. On the growth end: the capital-light, cash-generative core of the AI buildout — the foundry, the sold-out memory, the utilities with signed power contracts — sized with respect for how concentrated and how circular parts of this trade have become.

What we're deliberately underweighting is the middle: yield that's really token emissions in disguise, transaction counts that don't convert to dollars, and neocloud equity whose growth is financed by its own suppliers. The soft CPI relief rally is a fine moment to trim the narrative-heavy names into strength rather than chase them. Get paid, stay diversified across rails, and demand that every position show you where the cash actually comes from.
